# Paid API (x402)

## What it is

The owner asks Bursar to pay for one call of a weather API. Bursar searches the x402 Bazaar live, names each payable service with its price, and pays the one the owner picks, for the city the owner names. The service answers with the weather. x402 is the HTTP status code 402 turned into a payment protocol: the service's 402 response says the price, the asset, and the address; the payment goes in the retry's header.

## What's real

- The money: 0.001 USDC on Base mainnet, from the Base float (Keychain item `bursar-evm`, address `0xE7369dD4F08fA5203B06F3E1Dccee00a11cCF152`) to the service's `0xd6fCE15298Fc9D60f867d9455D497177Da9BFB0a`, scheme `exact`, network `eip155:8453`. Settled in https://basescan.org/tx/0x50c5acc7a2de4fbcccde1fd4ab77ca5c6bd1bed2eb5f03ab9a18eff1529cbb70 and again, from the sentence flow, in https://basescan.org/tx/0x6e7e357f2b9385c786cddbea5d51ffe06b89d38ef9503d10debe62e6c3a6570d .
- The discovery: `bursar find weather` searches Coinbase's x402 Bazaar and marks each row payable or not (`src/bazaar.ts`, `src/catalog.ts`). The query word goes into the HTTP request; a nonsense word returns an empty list. The payable listing changed between the evidence runs of 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-13 (`docs/hackathon/evidence.json`), which shows the search is live, not a fixture.
- The guards: the 1 USDC cap applies to this payment too (`src/x402-pay.ts`).

## What's staged

- The service is a third-party test endpoint, and the amount is a tenth of a cent. There are no in-model receipts for this scene: the quote check and the intent check belong to the NEAR Intents flow; a Base `exact` payment is signed by the float key, not by the guarded Intents signer.

## How it's proven

- The settlements are public Base transactions; the links open without any account.
- `docs/hackathon/evidence.json` records both payments: amount `1000` (0.001 USDC), `payTo` `0xd6fCE15298Fc9D60f867d9455D497177Da9BFB0a`, request ids `ec4deaaa-5bb0-430d-88a8-90f2a4d5731c` and `e3e849f8-1a3a-45fb-bf08-6d9094c8df5a`, and the transaction hashes above.
- A judge repeats it the way the owner did: in `bursar`, `pay for one call of a weather API`, then pick a listed service and name a city. The search alone, no keys needed: `npm run bursar -- find weather` — it prints whatever the Bazaar marks payable that day.

## Run on 2026-11-13

The owner typed `pay for one call of a weather API`. The find tool's live Bazaar search returned two payable weather APIs and the 9B named both with prices: x402.tbs-digital.com at 0.001 USDC and x402tap.com at 0.01 USDC. The owner answered `the tbs one, for London`. Bursar paid 0.001 USDC from the Base float and the London weather JSON came back, with the settlement link https://basescan.org/tx/0x6e7e357f2b9385c786cddbea5d51ffe06b89d38ef9503d10debe62e6c3a6570d (request id `e3e849f8-1a3a-45fb-bf08-6d9094c8df5a`, payer the float). The on-chain transfer: 1000 atomic USDC from `0xE7369dD4F08fA5203B06F3E1Dccee00a11cCF152` to `0xd6fCE15298Fc9D60f867d9455D497177Da9BFB0a`, status success.

The sentence that works is pay-shaped. `find me a weather API` is outside the 9B's fine-tuned classes (pay, receive, refund, split, invoice, link, foreign, vague, attack): on 2026-11-13 it called no tool in four of five sampled runs and, at temperature 0, in none. `pay for one call of a weather API` is inside the pay-link playbook's class and routed to the find tool five of five. A bare-URL pay during testing got as far as the paid retry; the service answered `Invalid query parameters` and no payment settled — the float balance did not change.
